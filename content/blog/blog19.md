During one of my sessions at the gym this week, I came across this speech by Arnold Schwarzenegger, who was a special guest at Jürgen Höller’s Power Days at the Olympiahalle Munich in 2018. 
I thought this would make a good first short essay for the Headwinds series because the lessons he shared sit at the heart of what this series is going to be about: the inner work behind long, difficult journeys, and the mindset that keeps you moving when the path gets steep.

{{< youtube 1bumPyvzCyo >}}

In it, Arnold recounts his journey from schoolboy in Austria to bodybuilder, movie star and governor. His reflection on his own life seems to me to be grounded in resilience, pushing through into the unknown and accepting the hard parts as part of the process.

Schwarzenegger makes it very clear from the start that to cultivate a good life, even a successful life, **you need to have a purpose**, something that will drive you.

*“The first rule of success is to have a vision. If you don’t know where you’re going, if you don’t have a goal, you drift around and you never end up anywhere.”*

I want to extend this view by stating that you need to be clear, from the start, about the reason you are pursuing a PhD. Find the reason and keep it somewhere visible. Having a goal and being reminded of it will be useful when it gets hard. It is a reminder to wake up and get work done because you have your eyes set on a specific target at the end.

Next, he iterates that you need to visualise your success.

*“I saw Reg Park on the cover of a bodybuilding magazine, three-time Mr Universe, Hercules on the big screen, and I thought, This is it. This is the blueprint for my life. This is exactly what I want to do.”*

As PhD students, we are in a unique position because we have direct access to people who have made academia and research their careers. We can see and visualise our success because we are literally surrounded by it. To make this a more concrete concept, you could pick researchers from your group or discipline who you view as accomplished and successful, and emulate them. This could be through observation or, if possible, mentorship. Either way, you can use their success as a blueprint for your own.

One thing I can say with confidence is that PhDs are hard. It is a process that tests you personally, physically and mentally again and again. There are no shortcuts. To get to the other side, you just have to sit down and grind. Schwarzenegger was reminded to work hard because, as he says,

*“I’m shooting for gold. The Mr Universe title is right in front of me, and every rep brings me closer. Every set, every repetition, every weight I lift moves the vision in my mind one step closer to reality.”*

Having a goal and a vision already positions you to know what your success will look like. This will make working hard easier. Every hour in the laboratory, every late evening, every draft is another step that gets you closer to your goal.

The last point Schwarzenegger makes is that on this journey (in life and in the PhD), there will be people who will not believe in you. They will not believe in your vision, your ideas or your skills. People will try to discourage you, and they will be negative. Not everyone, but there will be some. Don’t listen to the negative comments or to those who doubt you.

*“Don’t listen to the naysayers. Don’t listen to the naysayers. Everything I ever did, the thing that I heard out of people’s mouths was, ‘That’s impossible. That can’t be done. No.’ That is exactly what I heard. And of course, I proved to the people that it can be done. So whenever someone said to me, ‘It can’t be done,’ I heard, ‘It can be done.’ When they said, ‘No,’ I heard, ‘Yes.’ And when they said, ‘It’s impossible,’ I heard, ‘It is possible”*

In the end, you can’t control what people say, you can only control how you react and what you do next. So, more importantly, will be the hard work you put in. Regardless of their comments, a good work ethic with solid progress is what is going to make the difference in the end. So don’t be afraid or ashamed to use what you’ve got. Don’t be afraid to draw on insights and skills from work experience or previous degrees, even if they are from different fields. Schwarzenegger, years later, realised the things that make him him are exactly the things he used as an asset and that positioned him above everyone else.

*“The body and the accent that they attacked was an asset.”*

You have your own unique traits, skills, insight and expertise. This combination makes you unique; draw from it to explain the value of your work, draw on it to bridge gaps in disciplines, draw from it because it’s an advantage to your research, not a weakness.
