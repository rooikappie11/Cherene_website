#### *The man who chases two rabbits, catches neither.*
---

This cautionary saying is attributed to the Chinese philosopher Confucius (551–479 BC). Fast forward 2,500 years, and PGRs and knowledge workers still scramble to hold onto the wisdom imparted to us by Confucius. This single sentence encapsulates so profoundly why we are struggling to do deep and meaningful work.

There are 2 lies that keep us from being productive. The first is that we need more than 24 hours a day to accomplish the things on our to-do lists. And secondly, we can only achieve these things by multitasking. Instead of encouraging us to spend the time we have more productively, these 2 lies contribute to our fractured focus. It is a vicious cycle, the Forever Fractured Focus Loop, that won’t get you anywhere until you break it.

![Forever Fractured Focus Loop](/blog/blog23_a.jpg) <br>
*The “Forever Fractured Focus Loop” can feel like an eternally unbreakable cycle. With a few actionable steps, you can interrupt the pattern and set yourself on a different, more productive path.*<br>

Below, I will present three actionable steps that you can take to ensure you meet your targets and achieve the things you need to in order to succeed in PhD life.

The three steps are:

1. Time block your day.

2. Focus on one task at a time.

3. Avoid distractions at all costs.

These 3 ideas are simple. They are not even original. But if implemented together, it will allow you, in the words of [Cal Newport](https://calnewport.com/on-confronting-the-productivity-dragon-take-2/), “to beat the productivity dragon.”

PhD life is hard and demanding. PGRs often find themselves juggling too many balls, endlessly wishing they had more hands and more time. The thing is, we have all the time we need. 
The problem is that we are spending it unwisely, wasting hours as they tick by. But to master the PhD, you need to master each ball one at a time. This means dedicating a specific amount of time in your day to focus on one specific task at a time. [Time blocking](https://www.mural.co/blog/time-blocking) is a wonderful tool because you can automate your day first thing in the morning. This means you start off with a manageable and concrete work strategy. With a plan on paper, you won’t flounder around aimlessly after lunch. It will require discipline. If you do manage to stick to it, you can knock items off your list, like catching one ball at a time.

Once you have a time block plan in place, make sure that you dedicate the amount of time that is reasonable to complete the task. 
Develop and maintain a daily list or board with tasks, their priority and status. 
This will allow you to focus your energy on high-priority tasks while leaving less important tasks until you have capacity (mentally and physically) to deal with them. 
Seeing how busy you are or will be in the coming weeks will allow you to judge whether you can realistically commit to additional activities. In times of stress, when you are under pressure, avoid the urge to [multitask](https://www.mindlabpro.com/blogs/nootropics/is-multitasking-a-myth-why-less-means-more-when-it-comes-to-productivity?srsltid=AfmBOoqV2QXTNZPD34X_SRI0qlzbCNzI5sPG2jq2d0xbDwihEHkRLjOQ).
Humans are not good multitaskers. It divides our cognitive attention. Focusing on more than one thing also means you are less likely to give each of the tasks the time and dedicated attention needed to produce quality work.

This brings us to my last point: above all, avoid distractions. You phone, extended coffee breaks, and opening and refreshing your email. 
These might seem like harmless 5-minute breaks, but they fracture your focus and [interupt](https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbescoachescouncil/2022/05/16/mitigating-the-distraction-of-notifications-and-other-interruptions-at-work/) your momentum and the rhythm required for deep work. If possible, keep your phone in your bag or in a desk drawer. If you need to meet a friend, schedule a coffee catch-up so that it is included in your daily planning. You can also pick a time to dedicate 30 minutes of the day to tackle all your emails. You are allowed to be a little selfish. A PhD allows that sort of flexibility.

PGRs are in a unique position to manage and schedule their work day to tackle parts of the PhD piece by piece. 
Distractions cost time. And time well spent becomes the backbone of every dissertation.

PhDs might be hard, but they also go by fast. Once you integrate these three steps, you will maximise the time you spend during office hours to focus on the tasks and skills that matter for the final Viva and dissertation. This also means you don’t have to dedicate all of your evenings to work. Leaving you free to explore the city, meet up with friends or go for long walks.
