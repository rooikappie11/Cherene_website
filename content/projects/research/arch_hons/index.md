### Research Description
The Waterberg region in the Limpopo Province of South Africa is an area rich in cultural
histories and archaeological sites. The Masebe Nature Reserve is situated in this region. 
This project research was conducted on the stone walls and potsherds recorded on the Iron Age hill settlement next to the Thabana ya Kgosi hill. Data
was gathered through detailed sampling strategies to determine the spatial
distribution and variation of the ceramics on the site. Historical sources and archaeological
data indicated that the site was occupied multiple times. The Eiland ceramics found on the site
marks the first occupation which dates to AD 1000-1300 and was followed by Undecorated
and Moloko ceramics of Sotho-Tswana and Nguni people from AD 1350 - 1500. Ceramics from the Moor Park 
tradition indicated that the site was later occupied by the Northern Transvaal Langa Ndebele from
around AD 1854. The identity of the occupants and the socio-historical context of the site
was investigated through the material culture found at the site and the local oral histories
in the region. This study contributed to the larger body of knowledge related to Iron Age
settlement in the Waterberg region.
